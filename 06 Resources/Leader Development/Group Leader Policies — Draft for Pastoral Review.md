## The Heart Behind These Policies

Group leaders are shepherds of a small flock, not security guards, counselors, or investigators. These policies exist so leaders never have to carry a heavy moment alone or improvise in a crisis. In every situation the posture is the same: **keep people safe, stay present, and bring in pastoral help.** Policies protect the vulnerable, the leader, and the trust that lets a group become a place of belonging.

## Part 1 — Urgent Situations

### 1. Medical Emergency or Immediate Danger

**Proposed policy:** When anyone's life or safety is in immediate danger, leaders call 911 first, then notify the church.

**What leaders do:**

1. Call **911**. Do not wait for permission or try to handle a serious situation alone.
2. Stay with the person if it is safe to do so; keep others calm and clear of danger.
3. When the situation is stable, notify **Russ (primary pastoral contact)** the same day, or **Paul (backup contact)** if the primary cannot be reached.
4. Write down only the basic facts (what happened, when, who responded) and give them to the pastoral contact. Do not post details in a group chat.

**For Russ:** For now, Russ is the primary pastoral contact and Paul the backup. Please confirm, and note any incident form or record the church already uses.

### 2. Suspected Abuse or Neglect of a Child or Vulnerable Adult

**Proposed policy:** Any leader who learns of or suspects abuse or neglect of a child or a vulnerable adult reports it promptly, does not investigate, and does not promise to keep it secret.

**What leaders do:**

1. If anyone is in immediate danger, call **911**.
2. Listen calmly. Do not ask leading questions, press for details, or confront the person suspected.
3. Do not promise to keep it confidential; say instead, "I care about you, and I need to get you help."
4. Contact **Russ (primary pastoral contact)** the same day.
5. Make or support a report to the authorities as the church's approved procedure requires. In Colorado, child abuse and neglect can be reported to the statewide hotline at **1-844-CO-4-KIDS (1-844-264-5437)** *(verify before publishing)*. A report to the church never replaces a report the law requires.
6. Record only the facts given and the actions taken, and hand them to the pastoral contact. Keep them out of chats, email threads, and this repository.

**For Russ:**

- Which roles at Golden City Church are legally required to report, and does the church ask *all* group leaders to report regardless? A conservative and common church practice is "if in doubt, report."
- Who makes or supports the report, and how quickly?
- Which vulnerable-adult reporting resource should leaders use?

### 3. Risk of Suicide or Self-Harm

**Proposed policy:** When someone speaks of ending their life or harming themselves, leaders take it seriously every time, stay with them, and connect them to help.

**What leaders do:**

1. If there is immediate risk, call **911** and stay with the person.
2. Ask directly and gently whether they are thinking about suicide. Asking does not plant the idea.
3. Share the **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** (call or text **988**) and offer to make the call with them.
4. Do not leave someone at immediate risk alone, and do not promise secrecy.
5. Notify **Russ (primary pastoral contact)** the same day.
6. Follow up with care afterward, without making the leader the person's only support.

**For Russ:** Should leaders receive a short training segment on responding to suicide risk before groups launch, or soon after?

### 4. Disclosure of Domestic Violence or Abuse at Home

**Proposed policy:** When someone discloses violence or abuse at home, leaders put the person's safety first and connect them to pastoral and professional help. Leaders never confront the person accused or arrange a meeting between them.

**What leaders do:**

1. If there is immediate danger, call **911**.
2. Believe and listen; do not tell the person what they must do.
3. Share the **National Domestic Violence Hotline** (**1-800-799-7233**, or text **START** to **88788**) *(verify before publishing)*.
4. Notify **Russ (primary pastoral contact)** the same day. If a child is involved, follow Policy 2.
5. Do not use Scripture or prayer to pressure the person to stay, reconcile, or stay silent (Group Boundaries, DEC-028).

**For Russ:** Does the church have a pastoral approach to marriage counsel in abuse situations that leaders should know about?

## Part 2 — Care and Confidentiality

### 5. Confidentiality

**Proposed policy:** What is shared in a group stays in the group, with clear exceptions. Leaders never promise absolute confidentiality.

**What leaders do:**

- Early in the season, tell the group plainly: "What's shared here stays here, unless someone is in danger, a child or vulnerable person may be harmed, or the law requires us to tell someone."
- Do not repeat a person's story outside the group without their permission, including to other leaders, except to the pastoral contact when care requires it.
- Keep identifiable pastoral details out of group chats, social media, and personal notes (DEC-028).

### 6. Sharing Prayer Requests

**Proposed policy:** A prayer request belongs to the person who shares it. Leaders and members ask before passing it on.

**What leaders do:**

- Ask, "Is it okay to share this with the group?" or "Can I pass this along to the pastors?"
- In group chats, share only what the person has agreed to share.
- Move sensitive requests (health, marriage, legal, mental-health) to private, pastoral follow-up rather than the group thread.

### 7. When Care Needs More Than the Group Can Give

**Proposed policy:** Groups care for people through presence, prayer, and practical help, and they connect people to pastors and professionals when a need goes beyond that (DEC-028). Leaders are not counselors, therapists, or crisis responders.

**What leaders do:**

- **Grief, illness, or hospitalization:** let **Russ (primary pastoral contact)** know (with the person's permission) so a pastor can follow up; organize meals or visits if the person welcomes them.
- **Ongoing emotional, marital, addiction, or mental-health struggles:** pray and stay connected, and refer the person to a pastor or a qualified professional.
- **Spiritual or emotional crisis in a gathering:** pause, care for the person privately with another leader present if possible, and follow up with the pastoral contact.

**For Russ:** Does the church keep a list of trusted counselors or care resources leaders can refer people to?

### 8. Financial Needs and Requests for Money

**Proposed policy:** Leaders do not give or promise church funds. Requests for financial help go to the church's pastoral team (Group Boundaries).

**What leaders do:**

- Listen with care and refer the person to **Russ (primary pastoral contact)**.
- A group may choose to bless someone together (a meal, groceries, a gift card), but leaders should not pressure members to give or collect money for anyone without church approval.
- If someone asks multiple members for money, let the pastoral contact know.

**For Russ:** Does the church have, or plan, a benevolence process? Should groups be free to take up their own collections for a member in need?

## Part 3 — Group Life

### 9. Difficult or Disruptive Behavior

**Proposed policy:** Leaders love people well, including people who are hard to be with, while protecting the group so others can belong.

**What leaders do:**

1. **Dominating the conversation:** redirect warmly ("Let's hear from someone who hasn't shared yet"); if it continues, talk privately and kindly.
2. **Repeated disruption, divisive talk, or pushing an agenda:** speak privately with the person, ideally with a co-leader present; name the concern and the hope.
3. **If it continues:** involve Paul. A pastor may need to meet with the person.
4. **Threatening, abusive, or unsafe behavior:** see Policy 1 and contact the pastoral contact immediately. Leaders may ask a person to leave a gathering if others' safety is at risk.

Leaders do not remove someone from a group on their own; removing someone is decided with Paul and, where needed, Russ.

See also [[06 Resources/Group Life/Conflict|Conflict]] and [[02 Group Philosophy/Healthy Groups/Loving Messy People|Loving Messy People]].

### 10. Conflict Between Members

**Proposed policy:** Conflict is handled the way Scripture teaches: directly, privately first, and with grace (Matthew 18:15–17), and never as gossip in the group or the group chat.

**What leaders do:**

- Encourage members to go to each other first.
- If it does not resolve, the leader may help the two meet together.
- If it involves the leader, or if it escalates, bring in Paul.

### 11. Teaching, Disputed Doctrine, and Guest Speakers

**Proposed policy:** City Group leaders choose their group's material (DEC-052). Teaching stays within Golden City Church's beliefs, and disputed or divisive teaching moves to pastoral review (DEC-021).

**What leaders do:**

- Choose material freely, and ask Paul if unsure whether something fits.
- When a question comes up the leader cannot answer, say so and bring it to Paul or a pastor rather than guessing.
- Do not invite outside speakers or teachers to lead the group without Paul's okay.
- Do not use the group for political campaigning, fundraising, or selling products (Group Boundaries).

### 12. Leader Conduct and Appropriate Boundaries

**Proposed policy:** Leaders model integrity, protect people from harm, and avoid situations that could hurt someone or be misunderstood.

**What leaders do:**

- Treat group members with respect; never use a leader's role for personal, romantic, financial, or business advantage.
- Avoid extended one-on-one time alone with a member of the opposite sex, especially in private settings; meet in public places or bring another person along.
- In Collectives, men care for men and women care for women; cross-gender pastoral follow-up goes through a co-leader of the same gender or a pastor.
- Leaders who become aware of a fellow leader's misconduct tell **Russ (primary pastoral contact)**.
- A leader going through a hard season should feel free to step back without shame (DEC-061).

**For Russ:** How strict should the one-on-one guidance be? Options range from "avoid when practical" to "never meet one-on-one in private with someone of the opposite sex." Who handles concerns about a leader's conduct?

## Part 4 — Gatherings and Logistics

### 13. Meeting in Private Homes

**Proposed policy:** A home gathering is welcoming, safe, and honest about what people can expect. Applicable safeguards are confirmed before a group meets in a private home (DEC-028).

**What hosts do:**

- Keep the space reasonably safe: clear walkways, working lighting, pets secured or disclosed in advance for those with allergies or fears.
- Tell people in advance about stairs, parking, and accessibility.
- Never publish the home address. Share it privately with members who join (DEC-059).
- Know the nearest exits and keep a phone available.
- Let Paul know of any safety concern with the home or its surroundings.

**For Russ:** Should the church confirm with its insurance provider whether off-site home gatherings are covered, and whether hosts need to know anything?

### 14. Alcohol at Gatherings

**Proposed policy:** *Needs Russ's direction.* Churches commonly choose one of these:

- **Option A:** No alcohol at any group gathering.
- **Option B:** No alcohol at regular gatherings; leaders may allow it in moderation at social events, never where minors are present or anyone in recovery would be affected.
- **Option C:** Hosts decide, within moderation, with nothing served to minors.

**For Russ:** Which option fits Golden City Church? Option A or B is the most common for official ministry gatherings.

### 15. Children at Gatherings and Childcare

**Proposed policy:** Parents are responsible for their own children at group gatherings unless the church has approved a childcare arrangement. Groups do not run their own childcare without church approval (Group Boundaries).

**What leaders do:**

- Tell families in advance whether children are welcome and how they will be supervised.
- If a group wants to share childcare (for example, hiring a sitter together), get approval first so the church's child-safety requirements apply.
- Never leave a child alone with one unrelated adult; use a two-adult rule for any supervised children.
- A future Youth City Group would need the church's full child-safety requirements before it begins (DEC-028).

**For Russ:** What child-safety requirements (screening, training, two-adult rule) apply to anyone supervising children at a group gathering?

### 16. Transportation

**Proposed policy:** Groups do not arrange church-sponsored transportation without approval. Members who carpool do so as private individuals.

**What leaders do:**

- Do not drive minors who are not your own unless a parent is present or the church's child-safety rules allow it.
- Do not ask anyone to drive members as a church responsibility.

### 17. Photos, Social Media, and Group Chats

**Proposed policy:** Group members' privacy is protected online.

**What leaders do:**

- Ask before taking or posting photos of members; never post photos of minors without a parent's permission.
- Keep group chats for logistics, encouragement, and agreed prayer requests; keep sensitive details out (DEC-028, DEC-057).
- Do not share members' contact information outside the group without permission.
- Do not add people to a group chat without their agreement.

### 18. Cancellations, Leader Absence, and Stepping Down

**Proposed policy:** Members can count on clear communication, and leaders can count on the ministry's support.

**What leaders do:**

- If a gathering is cancelled, tell the group as early as possible through the group's usual channel.
- If a leader will miss several weeks, arrange for a co-leader to lead or let Paul know.
- A leader who needs to step down talks with Paul first; together they plan care for the group.
- Few sign-ups or uneven attendance are never a reason a leader has failed (DEC-061).

## Part 5 — Sensitive Attendance

### 19. A Person Whose Presence Raises a Safety Concern

**Proposed policy:** Everyone is welcome to belong, but safety comes first where there is a known risk. A leader who learns that someone attending may pose a risk, such as a known history of harming children or of violence, tells **Russ (primary pastoral contact)** promptly and does not handle it alone.

**What leaders do:**

- Do not confront, announce, or gossip about the concern.
- Contact the pastoral contact the same day; the church decides whether and how the person may take part.

**For Russ:** Does the church have, or want, a policy for welcoming people with known offense histories (for example, an attendance agreement)? This is most important for any gathering where children are present.
