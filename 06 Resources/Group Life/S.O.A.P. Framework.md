## S.O.A.P.

*A framework for reading, studying, and living out the Bible.*

### S – Scripture

What is the literary style of the passage? (narrative, poetry, wisdom literature, prophecy, gospel, epistle)

Slow down and read in context. Pay attention to the key words, grammar, repetition, and structure.

### O – Observation

What is happening in the passage? (who, what, where, when, and why)

What does the passage reveal about God, humanity, and God's relationship with His people?

Where does this passage stand in the story of redemption?

### A – Application

How is God shaping my heart, desires, or character through this passage?

How can I embed this biblical truth into daily rhythms?

### P – Prayer

We are answerable subjects before the Word of God, and prayer is our faithful response.

Praise God for who He is and what He has done.

Ask the Holy Spirit to help me live by faith, grow in obedience, and cultivate hope in Christ.

## How the Collectives Use It

The Collective gathering moves from objective biblical truth to subjective personal reflection and application:

| S.O.A.P. | In the gathering | In the handout |
|---|---|---|
| **Scripture** | Open Bibles to the chapter; notice its literary style and structure | Scripture: the passage, its genre, its movements, and words to watch |
| **Observation** | A short teaching or devotional in each of the two blocks (DEC-085) | Part 1 and Part 2 teaching summaries, with Discuss questions that begin in the text |
| **Application** | Discussion in each block, with the larger share of its time, at tables when attendance calls for it | The second Discuss question in each part, and Reflections |
| **Prayer** | Regroup for final thoughts and prayer requests, then pray out | Close Prayer |

The [[06 Resources/Curriculum Development/Complexity Gate|Complexity Gate]] sets how deep each movement goes.
