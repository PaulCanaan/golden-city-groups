# Patience

**Weekly Recap · October 25, 2026** · *The Fruit of the Spirit* · Galatians 5:22–23; Romans 5:1–5

## Check In

1. What is something you had to wait for, big or small, that turned out to be worth it?
2. Where did your week call for a little extra patience?

## This Week's Message

Sunday's message, from a guest preacher, turned to the fourth fruit of the Spirit, patience, under the title "The Weight of Waiting." We live in a culture that wants everything now: the win, the promotion, the perfect life, even the growth we long to see in our church. But patience isn't accepting defeat; it is trusting God's timeline when everything in us wants our own. Walking through Romans 5:1–5, the message traced three movements. Patience is active: we see it in Jesus, who regarded others as more important than himself, and in Romans 5 it is part of how God produces endurance, character, and hope. Patience transforms us: God's love has been poured into our hearts through the Spirit, and as his chosen and dearly loved people we are called to put on patience (Colossians 3:12). And patience is God's model, seen at the cross (2 Corinthians 5:21). The Hebrew idea behind it is "to be long," slow to anger. The waiting isn't the obstacle; it's where transformation happens.

## Read Together

Galatians 5:22–23 and Romans 5:1–5

## Discuss

1. What stands out to you in Romans 5:1–5?
2. Paul says affliction produces endurance, endurance produces character, and character produces hope, a hope resting on God's love poured into our hearts through the Spirit. Where have you seen God grow something in you through a season of waiting?
3. The message pointed to Jesus as our model of patience: he regarded others as more important than himself and went to the cross for us. How does God's patience with you in Christ shape the way you treat the people who test your patience?
4. "The waiting isn't the obstacle; it's where transformation happens." Where are you waiting on God right now, and what might get in the way of trusting his timeline?

## Pray

Thank God for his patience with us, shown most fully at the cross. Ask him to pour his love into your hearts again through the Spirit (Romans 5:5), since we can't give what we haven't received, and pray for one another, especially for anyone in a long season of waiting.
