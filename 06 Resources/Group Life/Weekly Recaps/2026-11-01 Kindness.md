# Kindness

**Weekly Recap · November 1, 2026** · *The Fruit of the Spirit* · Week 5

## Check In

1. What's a small action of kindness someone did for you that you've never forgotten?
2. Where did kindness show up in your week, giving it or receiving it?

## This Week's Message

Sunday's message turned to kindness, the fifth fruit of the Spirit, which is not something we work up, but something the Spirit grows in us. It traced four movements. God was kind first: Titus 3 gives our "before" picture, but when God's kindness appeared in Christ, He saved us according to His mercy, not our works. As David sought out Mephibosheth, Jonathan's lame son, to keep a promise and seat him at his table, God came looking for us, and we can't give what we've never received. Kindness changes hearts: God's kindness leads us to repentance (Romans 2:4), and Jesus' kindness brought Peter home after his denial. Kindness needs a clean heart: bitterness can't be covered up, only removed, before kindness can be put on (Ephesians 4:31–32). And kindness crosses the road: in the Good Samaritan, the question isn't who deserves our kindness, but whether we'll stop. The table covers what we can't fix; so let go of what needs to fall, and cross the road for someone this week.

## Read Together

Titus 3:3–5

## Discuss

1. What stands out to you in Titus 3:3–5?
2. David went looking for Mephibosheth, and God came looking for us. What does it do to you, knowing His kindness came first?
3. Romans 2:4 says God's kindness, not His anger, leads us to repentance. When has His kindness drawn you back to Him?
4. Who is one person on the other side of your road this week? And what might get in the way of stopping for them?

## Pray

1. Thank God His kindness came first, that He saved us in Christ because of His mercy.
2. Ask the Spirit to help us let go of what needs to fall, the grudges and bitterness we've been carrying, so kindness can grow in their place.
3. Ask Him to open our eyes to the person on the other side of the road this week.
4. Pray for one another, especially for anyone still carrying a hurt they can't let go of.
