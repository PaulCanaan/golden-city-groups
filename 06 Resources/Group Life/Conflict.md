# Conflict and Difficult Conversations

## Document Status

**Proposed leader resource.** Correction authority, formal discipline, abuse reporting, and removal procedures require pastoral approval and church-wide policy.

## Ministry Purpose

Help leaders preserve truth, peace, dignity, participation, and safety when disagreement or harmful behavior appears in group life.

## In-the-Moment Response

1. Stay calm and listen for the actual issue.
2. Clarify misunderstanding before assigning motive.
3. Redirect conversation that becomes divisive, demeaning, speculative, monopolizing, or detached from Scripture and the group's purpose.
4. Protect participants from public interrogation, humiliation, or forced disclosure.
5. Pause the discussion when the matter cannot be handled safely or faithfully in the group.
6. Arrange an appropriate private follow-up and contact the approved ministry leader when needed.

## Private Follow-Up

- Pray and review what happened before the conversation.
- Speak directly and specifically about observed words or behavior.
- Listen to the person's perspective.
- Restate the group's purpose and approved boundaries.
- Agree on a clear next step where the issue is within the leader's authority.
- Document only the minimum approved operational outcome.

## Escalate Immediately

Do not attempt ordinary conflict resolution when there is imminent danger, suspected abuse, threats of harm, stalking, coercion, harassment, illegal activity, a safeguarding concern, or a crisis beyond the leader's role. Use the church-approved reporting and pastoral escalation process.

## What Leaders Must Not Do

- promise secrecy
- conduct an investigation beyond their role
- diagnose mental-health, medical, legal, marital, or financial problems
- mediate alone where safety, power imbalance, abuse, or serious misconduct may be present
- use prayer or Scripture to silence a person, avoid accountability, or force reconciliation
- remove a participant or leader unless the approved authority permits it

## Formation Relationship

- **Belonging:** honest repair protects community from false peace and exclusion.
- **Beholding:** truth, grace, repentance, and reconciliation remain centered on Christ.
- **Becoming:** participants practice patience, courage, forgiveness, boundaries, and faithful accountability.

## Responsibility and Approval

Leaders may facilitate ordinary discussion and relational repair within their approved role. Paul or the named pastoral contact owns situations requiring correction, pause, removal, formal discipline, safeguarding, or referral.
