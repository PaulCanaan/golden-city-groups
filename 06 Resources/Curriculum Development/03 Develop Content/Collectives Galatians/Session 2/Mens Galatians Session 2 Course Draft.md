## Teaching Block 1 — A Servant of Christ

### 6:50–6:54 — Review, not a second overview

Do not reteach the map of the letter. Three or four sentences are enough.

Last gathering we stayed in Galatians 1:1–9. Paul writes as an apostle of the risen Jesus. Jesus gave himself for our sins to rescue us, according to the Father's will. Paul is alarmed because the churches are turning toward a different gospel. Adding something else as the basis of acceptance before God distorts the gospel of Christ. Even an apostle stands under that gospel.

Tonight the letter answers a suspicion that follows that warning: maybe Paul shaped the message to please people.

### 6:54–7:00 — Read 1:10–14

**Read 1:10–14 aloud.** Ask what Paul refuses, and what his former zeal actually did. Take one or two short observations, then teach.

Verse 10 is sharp. Is he trying to persuade people, or God? Is he striving to please people? If that were still his aim, he would not be a servant of Christ. "Still" matters. He is not claiming he has never cared what people think. He is saying that a servant of Christ cannot aim the gospel at winning approval.

Some of the Galatian teachers may have treated his law-free welcome of Gentiles as a softer message, designed to be popular. Paul denies that. The gospel is not made easier by removing Christ. It is not made truer by adding a qualification Christ did not require.

Verses 11–12 locate the message. It is not of human origin. He did not receive it from a human source, and he was not taught it as something he constructed. It came by a revelation of Jesus Christ. Hold the boundary from Session 1: this is apostolic witness. A leader now does not claim Paul's authority through a private experience. We receive this gospel in Scripture.

### 7:00–7:10 — Zeal is not the gospel

Verses 13–14 keep us from romanticizing Paul's past. He intensely persecuted God's church and tried to destroy it. He advanced beyond many contemporaries because he was extremely zealous for the traditions of his ancestors. Say this carefully. Judaism is not the villain of the letter, and God's law is not a mistake. The text shows something nearer to us: a serious religious life, full of effort and progress, can still oppose the grace of Christ.

For the men in this room, the pressure may not look like persecution. It may look like needing to be the competent one, the certain one, the man who does not need help. That hunger can sand down what we say about Jesus. It can also keep us quiet about sin, fear, or confusion, because the truth would spoil the impression. Serving Christ frees a man to tell the truth. The gospel does not depend on the room being impressed with him.

## Teaching Block 2 — Called by Grace

### 7:20–7:26 — Read 1:15–24

**Read 1:15–24 aloud.** Ask who the subject of verses 15–16 is, and what the churches do in verse 24.

The turn is "But when God." God set Paul apart from his mother's womb, called him by grace, and was pleased to reveal his Son in him, so that he could preach Christ among the Gentiles. The call is grace. The advancement in verse 14 did not purchase it. God reveals his Son, and the purpose reaches the nations. That purpose will matter for the whole letter: Gentiles belong by the gospel, not by taking on an extra condition.

Paul did not immediately consult with anyone. He did not go up to Jerusalem to those who were apostles before him. He went to Arabia and came back to Damascus. Three years later he spent fifteen days with Cephas and saw James, the Lord's brother. He declares before God that he is not lying. Teach the reason. The churches need to know his gospel came from Christ, not from a strategy to please either Jerusalem or the Gentile churches.

Do not turn verse 17 into a solitary Christianity. Paul is defending the source of the apostolic gospel. In the next chapter he will lay that gospel before the other apostles. Brothers need one another. What they do not need is a message revised so the room will applaud.

### 7:26–7:40 — They glorified God

Paul then went to Syria and Cilicia. The Judean churches did not know him by face. They kept hearing a sentence: the man who formerly persecuted us now preaches the faith he once tried to destroy. And they glorified God because of him.

Sit on that ending. Paul's story is not a reputation he curates. The churches who only hear of him respond with worship. A changed life is a reason to praise God. Some men would rather edit the past, or present a record that needs no mercy: the dependable one, the one who has always had it together. Grace makes another kind of speech possible. We can say what we were, and what Christ has done, and let the glory go to God.

If a man tells a hard truth at the table, thank him and do not turn it into a spectacle. Point the table toward Christ. The practice this week is small: tell one brother something true, and ask for prayer. That step does not earn God's acceptance. It answers the grace that has already called him.

## Practice, Prayer, and Close — 7:50–8:00 PM

**Practice, as on the handout:** Reread Galatians 1:10–24. When you notice yourself shaping what you say so that someone will be impressed, return to the God who called Paul by grace. Tell one brother something true about your need or about Christ's mercy, and ask him to pray.

**Prayer:** Father, thank you for calling us by grace and for revealing your Son. Free us from living to impress one another. Where our zeal has been aimed at the wrong glory, turn us back to Christ. Teach us to tell the truth and to give you the praise for every change you work. Amen.
