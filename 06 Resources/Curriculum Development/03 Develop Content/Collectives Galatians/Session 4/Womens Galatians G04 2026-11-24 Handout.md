# Galatians 4

Known by God · Children of promise

## Last Session Review

In Galatians 3, the churches received the Spirit by believing, not by keeping up, just as Abraham “believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness” (3:2–6). Christ bore the law’s curse so that Abraham’s blessing reaches the nations (3:13–14). Those baptized into Christ are clothed with Him, and every woman in Christ is a full heir (3:27–29). A sister’s season gives her no larger or smaller share.

## Scripture

Open your Bibles to **Galatians 4:1–31**. Galatians is a letter, and in chapter 4 Paul’s case turns personal. An heir who is still a child is no different from a slave, until God sends His Son and gives us adoption and the Spirit (4:1–11). Then Paul pleads like a mother in labor and reads Sarah and Hagar as a picture of two covenants (4:12–31). Watch for *slave*, *heir*, *known*, and *free*.

### Part 1 · Known by God

Galatians 4:1–11

A child heir lives under guardians, no different from a slave, though the estate is hers (4:1–2). So were we, enslaved under the elements of the world (4:3). Then “God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the law, to redeem those under the law, so that we might receive adoption as sons” (4:4–5). In Paul’s world the son received the inheritance; in Christ every woman here receives that full share (3:28–29). He sent the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying, “Abba, Father!” (4:6). Yet the Galatians, now “known by God,” were turning back to “the weak and worthless elements,” keeping special days as if those could secure their place (4:9–10). Our measures may be a full calendar or keeping pace with another woman’s season. None adds to being known by God, so we can set them down.

## Discuss

1. What has God done for us through His Son and His Spirit (4:4–7)? What does it mean to you that you are known by God (4:9)?
2. What checklists or measures do you go back to when you want to feel secure? How does being His heir answer that pressure?

### Part 2 · Children of Promise

Galatians 4:12–31

The Galatians once welcomed Paul in weakness “as an angel of God” (4:14). Now others court them, “but not for good. They want to exclude you from me, so that you would pursue them” (4:17). Belonging becomes leverage, a circle you must keep earning. Paul’s love differs: “I am again suffering labor pains for you until Christ is formed in you” (4:19).

Then Paul turns to Sarah and Hagar: one son “born as a result of the flesh,” the other “born through promise” (4:23). He reads them as two covenants, not as a verdict on either woman’s worth; in Genesis, God saw Hagar and heard her son (16:13; 21:17). “The Jerusalem above is free, and she is our mother” (4:26). We are “children of promise” (4:28), so we can make room for one another at the table.

## Discuss

1. How can belonging be used as leverage (4:17)? How is Paul’s love for the Galatians different (4:19)?
2. We belong by promise, not by keeping up (4:26–28). How does that free you to welcome a sister without comparing?

## Reflections

1. Where am I trying to stay in a circle by keeping up, instead of resting in being known by God?
2. Which sister can I encourage this week toward Christ being formed in her?

## Close Prayer

Father, thank you for sending Your Son to redeem us and for making us Your heirs. Thank you that we are known by You and that Your Spirit teaches us to cry, “Abba, Father.” Forgive us where we have tried to earn our place by keeping up. Help us rest in Your promise and love our sisters with patience until Christ is formed in each of us. Amen.
