# Galatians 4

Sons, not slaves · Children of promise

## Last Session Review

In Galatians 3, the churches received the Spirit by believing, just as Abraham’s faith was credited to him for righteousness (3:2–6). Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us (3:13). The law could not cancel God’s promise; it guarded His people until Christ came (3:17, 24). All who belong to Christ are sons and heirs (3:26, 29), so a man need not re-earn his place or rank his brothers.

## Scripture

Open your Bibles to **Galatians 4:1–31**. Galatians is a letter, and in chapter 4 Paul’s case turns personal. An heir who is still a child is no different from a slave, until God sends His Son and gives us adoption and the Spirit (4:1–11). Then Paul pleads as a spiritual father and reads Abraham’s two sons as a picture of two covenants (4:12–31). Watch for *slave*, *son*, *heir*, and *free*.

### Part 1 · No Longer a Slave

Galatians 4:1–11

A child heir lives under guardians, no different from a slave, though the estate is his (4:1–2). So were we, enslaved under the elements of the world (4:3). Then “God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the law, to redeem those under the law, so that we might receive adoption as sons” (4:4–5). The Father did more than release us: He sent the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying, “Abba, Father!” (4:6). “So you are no longer a slave but a son” (4:7). Yet the Galatians, now “known by God,” were turning back to “the weak and worthless elements,” keeping special days as if those could secure their place (4:9–10). A man can relate to God like a hired hand: clock in, perform, hope the boss is satisfied. A son comes home and calls his father by name. Grace frees us to stop keeping score.

## Discuss

1. What did the Father do when the time came to completion (4:4–7)? What is the difference between being freed and being adopted?
2. Where do you relate to God more like a hired hand than a son? What old rules do men go back to when they want control?

### Part 2 · Children of Promise

Galatians 4:12–31

Paul stops arguing and pleads. They once welcomed him in weakness “as an angel of God” (4:14). Now he asks, “So then, have I become your enemy because I told you the truth?” (4:16). His love is costly: “I am again suffering labor pains for you until Christ is formed in you” (4:19). Brotherhood tells the truth and stays in the struggle.

Then Paul turns to Abraham. Ishmael “was born as a result of the flesh,” when Abraham and Sarah tried to secure God’s promise their own way; Isaac “was born through promise” (4:23; Genesis 16; 21). Paul reads them as two covenants, slavery from Sinai and freedom from “the Jerusalem above” (4:24–26). “Now you too, brothers and sisters, like Isaac, are children of promise” (4:28). Our place rests on what God promised, not on what we produce.

## Discuss

1. Paul risks being seen as an enemy to tell the truth (4:16, 19). What makes that kind of honesty between brothers possible?
2. Abraham and Sarah tried to make the promise happen themselves (4:23; Genesis 16:1–4). Where are you trying to produce by your own strength what God has promised to give?

## Reflections

1. Where do I live like a slave who must earn his keep instead of a son who is already known?
2. Which brother do I need to tell the truth to, or stay beside, until Christ is formed in him?

## Close Prayer

Father, thank you for sending Your Son when the time came to completion, to redeem us and make us Your sons. Thank you for the Spirit who teaches us to cry, “Abba, Father.” Forgive us for going back to old rules to stay in control. Give us courage to speak the truth in love and to stay beside our brothers until Christ is formed in us. We rest in Your promise. Amen.
