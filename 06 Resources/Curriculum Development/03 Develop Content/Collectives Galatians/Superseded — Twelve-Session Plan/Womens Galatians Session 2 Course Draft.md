## Session Formation Emphasis

**Proposed, continuing the reviewed Session 1 angle, not yet confirmed for this gathering:** receive Christ's grace as the ground of identity, and practice encouragement that gives God the glory instead of comparing stories.

- **Belonging:** A woman can be present with her actual story. Welcome does not depend on offering the more admirable one.
- **Beholding:** See the churches glorifying God because the persecutor now preaches the faith. The glory rests on God, who revealed his Son.
- **Becoming:** Tell the truth about mercy received, and encourage one woman by naming God's grace in her without measuring her against someone else.

Do not assume the women are wives, mothers, or comfortable speaking. Comparison and approval are this gathering's proposed angle, not a diagnosis of every woman.

## Teaching Block 1 — A Servant of Christ

### 6:50–6:54 — Review, not a second overview

Do not reteach the map of the letter.

Last gathering we stayed in Galatians 1:1–9. Jesus gave himself for our sins to rescue us. Paul is alarmed because the churches are turning from the God who called them by grace toward a different gospel. Adding a requirement as the basis of acceptance distorts the gospel of Christ. Even Paul stands under that gospel.

Tonight he answers the suspicion that he shaped the message to please people.

### 6:54–7:00 — Read 1:10–14

**Read 1:10–14 aloud.** Ask what Paul refuses, and what his former life produced. Take one or two observations, then teach.

Is he trying to persuade people, or God? Is he striving to please people? If pleasing people were still his aim, he would not be a servant of Christ. The gospel is not a message adjusted until the hearers are comfortable with the messenger. It came by a revelation of Jesus Christ. It is not of human origin.

Keep the boundary clear. Paul's revelation is apostolic. We are not waiting for a private revelation that bypasses Scripture. We receive the gospel he was given, in the apostolic word, and we refuse to reshape it so that we will be approved.

### 7:00–7:10 — Approval can sand down the truth

Verses 13–14 are unsparing. Paul persecuted God's church and tried to destroy it, even while he advanced beyond his contemporaries and burned with zeal for ancestral traditions. Jewish identity is not the enemy, and God's law is not the problem he is attacking. Zeal and progress did not lead him to Christ. They were aimed the other way until God acted.

Among us, the pressure is often quieter than persecution. A woman may want to sound assured, to keep the peace, or to be known as the steady one. None of those desires is evil in itself. They become a problem when they decide what she is willing to say about Jesus, or when another woman's apparent confidence becomes the measure of her own standing. Session 1 named comparison. This passage names the mechanism: pleasing people. Serving Christ frees her to tell the truth. Another woman's story does not make Jesus more or less sufficient.

## Teaching Block 2 — Called by Grace

### 7:20–7:26 — Read 1:15–24

**Read 1:15–24 aloud.** Ask who acts in verses 15–16, and whom the churches glorify in verse 24.

But when God. He set Paul apart, called him by grace, and was pleased to reveal his Son in him, so that he would preach Christ among the Gentiles. The sentence does not say that Paul finally became impressive enough. Grace called him. The Son was revealed. The purpose was proclamation to the nations, which is the letter's larger concern: Gentiles welcomed by the gospel itself.

Paul did not immediately consult with anyone or go to the apostles in Jerusalem. He went to Arabia and returned to Damascus. After three years he spent fifteen days with Cephas and saw James. He swears that he is not lying. Explain why the detail matters. The gospel's source is Christ, not a campaign to satisfy one party or another. Then protect the church: this is not an instruction to keep one's faith private or to avoid other believers. The next chapter shows Paul bringing his gospel into fellowship with the other apostles. We need one another. We do not need a message edited for applause.

### 7:26–7:40 — Glorify God because of me

The Judean churches did not know his face. They heard a report: the one who persecuted us now preaches the faith he tried to destroy. They glorified God because of him.

That is the end Paul wants. Not a testimony shaped until it compares well. Not a before-and-after arranged so the teller will be admired. Worship. When we tell what God has done, comparison returns easily: whose change sounds deeper, whose faith looks cleaner, who seems further along. Grace puts stories to a different use. We tell the truth about mercy, and we help a sister notice God's work in her without measuring it against ours.

If someone shares a tender thing, receive it and do not turn the table into an evaluation of whose story was better. The practice is specific: encourage one woman this week by naming what God has done, without comparing her to anyone else.

## Practice, Prayer, and Close — 7:50–8:00 PM

**Practice, as on the handout:** Reread Galatians 1:10–24. When you notice yourself shaping your story so it will compare well, return to the God who calls by grace. Encourage one woman this week by naming what God has done, without measuring her against someone else.

**Prayer:** Father, thank you for calling us by grace and for revealing your Son. Free us from shaping our stories for approval. Teach us to tell the truth about your mercy and to give you glory for your work in one another. By your Spirit, make our encouragement faithful and kind. Amen.
