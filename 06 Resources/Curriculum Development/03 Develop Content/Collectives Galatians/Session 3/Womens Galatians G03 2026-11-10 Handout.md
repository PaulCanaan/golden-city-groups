# Galatians 3

Blessed by faith · Heirs of the promise

## Last Session Review

In Galatians 2, Titus was welcomed without being compelled to become Jewish (2:3–5). The leaders recognized different callings serving one gospel: partnership, not competition (2:7–9). Then Peter quietly withdrew from the Gentile table because he feared others, and Paul challenged conduct that denied the gospel (2:11–14). We are justified, declared right with God, by faith in Christ, not by works of the law (2:16). The Son of God loved Paul and gave himself for him (2:20). That same love frees us from seeking one group’s approval and makes room for others at the table.

## Scripture

Open your Bibles to **Galatians 3:1–29**. Galatians is a letter: Paul is making a case to churches he loves, so follow his reasoning. Chapter 3 asks how God’s blessing comes: Paul points back to Abraham, who received righteousness by faith, and to Christ, who bore the law’s curse for us (3:1–14). Then he shows that the law never cancelled God’s promise; it guarded God’s people until Christ came, and now those who belong to Christ are his children and heirs (3:15–29). Watch for *faith*, *promise*, and *curse*.

### Part 1 · Received by Faith

Galatians 3:1–14

Paul brings the churches back to the crucified Christ. They received the Spirit by believing, yet now try to finish by the flesh (3:2–3). Abraham shows how God gives righteousness: he “believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness” (3:6). Abraham’s promised blessing was for the nations. It is received by faith, not achieved by keeping up. Relying on works of the law leaves us under its curse. Christ bore that curse for us so that Abraham’s blessing reaches the Gentiles and we receive the Spirit through faith (3:13–14). Comparison can become another attempt to complete what grace began. Another woman’s service, confidence, or season cannot supply what Christ has given. We can bring our need to him and encourage one another without measuring whose life looks stronger.

## Discuss

1. How did Abraham receive righteousness (3:6), and what reaches us through faith because of Christ (3:14)?
2. Where does comparison or keeping up become your way to finish by the flesh? How does Christ’s work answer that pressure?

### Part 2 · Clothed with Christ

Galatians 3:15–29

The law did not cancel God’s promise to Abraham. It came 430 years later, exposing transgressions and guarding God’s people until Christ (3:17–24). God gave the inheritance by promise. Those trusting Christ belong to God’s family. Those baptized into Christ are clothed with him (3:26–27). Their identity is given before anything they achieve.

Paul names Jew or Greek, slave or free, male and female: these divisions cannot determine our standing in Christ (3:28). Every woman belonging to Christ is a full heir of the promise (3:29). A sister’s different life or season does not give her a larger or smaller share. We can welcome her without asking how she compares with us. Encouragement grows as we help one another trust the Christ who clothes us and gives us our place.

## Discuss

1. What identity has Christ given us (3:26–27)? How does that answer the need to earn our place?
2. How does being one in Christ and fellow heirs (3:28–29) change how you see a sister whose life or season differs?

## Reflections

1. Where do I measure my standing by another woman’s life?
2. Who is one woman I can welcome or encourage this week as a fellow heir?

## Close Prayer

Father, thank you for giving us your Spirit through faith and clothing us with Christ. He bore the curse so we could share your promise. Forgive us where comparison has become our measure of belonging. Teach us to rest in the identity you give and to make room at the table. Help us welcome and encourage our sisters as fellow heirs. Amen.
