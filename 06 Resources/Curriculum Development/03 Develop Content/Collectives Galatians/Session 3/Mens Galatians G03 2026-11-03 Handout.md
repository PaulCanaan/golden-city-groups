# Galatians 3

Blessed by faith · Sons and heirs

## Last Session Review

In Galatians 2, Titus was not compelled to be circumcised: Gentiles belong to Christ without first becoming Jewish (2:3–5). In Antioch, Peter withdrew from the Gentile table because he feared others, and Paul corrected him publicly (2:11–14). Their conduct needed to match the gospel. We are justified, declared right with God, by faith in Christ, not by works of the law (2:16). Paul’s life now rested in Christ living in him (2:20). Brothers who share that grace can stand firm, tell the truth in love, and receive correction without protecting an image.

## Scripture

Open your Bibles to **Galatians 3:1–29**. Galatians is a letter: Paul is making a case to churches he loves, so follow his reasoning. Chapter 3 asks how God’s blessing comes: Paul points back to Abraham, who received righteousness by faith, and to Christ, who bore the law’s curse for us (3:1–14). Then he shows that the law never cancelled God’s promise; it guarded God’s people until Christ came, and now those who belong to Christ are his children and heirs (3:15–29). Watch for *faith*, *promise*, and *curse*.

### Part 1 · Beginning by the Spirit

Galatians 3:1–14

Paul is shocked. Christ was portrayed as crucified before them, yet they turn to their efforts. He asks, “After beginning by the Spirit, are you now finishing by the flesh?” (3:3). They received the Spirit by believing, and Abraham received righteousness the same way (3:2–6). God’s blessing was for the nations. Relying on works of the law brings a curse because we cannot keep it fully. But “Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us” (3:13). Through him we receive the promised Spirit by faith. We can begin with grace, then try to complete ourselves with effort, a record, or self-reliance. Christ’s cross frees us to admit need among brothers. We can ask for help without making strength or another man’s opinion our security.

## Discuss

1. How did they receive the Spirit (3:2, 5), and what does Abraham show (3:6)? How does Christ make this blessing ours (3:13–14)?
2. Where do effort, image, or self-reliance tempt you to finish by the flesh after receiving grace?

### Part 2 · Sons and Heirs

Galatians 3:15–29

God’s promise came before the law. The law arrived 430 years later and could not cancel God’s gracious gift to Abraham (3:15–18). It exposed transgressions and guarded God’s people until Christ came. A guardian serves children for a season; he cannot give their inheritance. Now that Christ has come, those who trust him are God’s sons and heirs (3:24–29).

A son does not re-earn his place each morning as if on probation. He belongs through Christ. That changes how we stand beside one another. Jew or Greek, slave or free, male and female do not determine anyone’s standing in Christ (3:28). Status, strength, and record cannot make one brother more entitled to God’s promise than another. We can lay down our rankings, receive correction, and encourage one another as fellow heirs.

## Discuss

1. In 3:24–29, what has Christ changed? How would living as a son and heir change a man who feels on probation?
2. Read 3:28. Which dividing lines do men use to rank one another? What does oneness in Christ ask of us?

## Reflections

1. Where do I act as if my standing with God must be re-earned?
2. Which brother could I encourage as a fellow heir this week, rather than a rival or project?

## Close Prayer

Father, thank you that Christ bore the curse for us and that we receive your Spirit through faith. Forgive our attempts to make our record or strength our security. Teach us to live as your sons, trusting your promise. Give us courage to admit need, receive help, and stand beside our brothers as fellow heirs. Keep our confidence in Jesus. Amen.
