## 1. Biblical Exegesis

- **1:1–2 — Who speaks, and to whom?** Paul identifies a commission originating with Jesus Christ and God the Father and names the resurrection. This concerns his apostolic commission; it does not give every current teacher the same authority through a private experience. The plural churches and accompanying brothers locate the letter in shared church life.
- **1:3–5 — What is the gospel's action?** Grace and peace arise from Christ's self-giving for sins and rescue from the present evil age, according to the Father's will. God initiates and accomplishes the rescue; God receives the glory. Faith receives this gift. The letter later develops the Spirit-shaped life that follows it.
- **1:6–7 — What is going wrong?** Paul is alarmed at people turning from the God who calls them in grace toward a distorted message. This is relational desertion as well as changed ideas. “Different” is not another equally valid rescue.
- **1:8–9 — What judges the messenger?** Even Paul's own future teaching and an impressive heavenly messenger are subject to the received gospel. The warning concerns a contrary gospel and God's judgment; it is not permission to attack a person, intimidate a questioner, or label every secondary disagreement heresy.

For the book overview, connect the crisis with the later discussion of Gentile inclusion, circumcision, law/promise, life in the Spirit, the cross, and new creation. Do not caricature Jewish people or imply that Scripture's law is evil. Explain the problem as making additional conditions the basis for belonging and righteousness before God.

## 2. Theological Synthesis

**Central idea:** Jesus gives himself to rescue sinners by grace; receive his gospel and test every proposed addition by it.

The resurrection in verse 1 and self-giving for sins in verse 4 keep Jesus at the center. The gospel announces God's action before it calls for our response. It humbles pride, gives hope to those who have failed, and frees people to grow in faithful obedience without making that growth the price of God's acceptance. Christ is the rescuer, not a supplement to self-salvation.

Keller's supplied notes guide the distinction between receiving rescue and trying to earn it, and between gospel revision and gospel reversal. The draft explains substitution as Jesus acting for sinners and bearing what they could not resolve; it does not present a novel church doctrinal position. Use the church's existing framework for grace, repentance, truth, the Spirit, and faithful community.

Keep historical explanation and present application distinct. The specific Galatian crisis is not identical to every contemporary feeling of pressure. Performance, approval, and comparison illustrate ways people may functionally seek security apart from Christ; ordinary responsibility, wise correction, and effort in discipleship remain good.

## 3. Proposed Formation Goals

Paul initially requested proposals, then reviewed the maintained Canvas synthesis and requested handout production from it. These are the session's selected working pastoral angles, not claims about all men or women; both sets of pressures can affect anyone.

| Dimension | Men's draft proposal | Women's draft proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Belonging | Practice honest brotherhood where needing help does not disqualify a man from being welcomed | Practice mutual encouragement where comparison, appearance, or a flawless faith story do not determine welcome |
| Beholding | Attend to Jesus as rescuer and receive his sufficient work instead of relying on a personal spiritual record | Attend to Christ's grace-given identity and receive his sufficient work instead of seeking comparative worth or approval |
| Becoming | Name one self-justifying habit and take a concrete step of truthful dependence, prayer, and mutual help | Name one approval/comparison habit and take a concrete step of gospel truth, prayer, and gracious encouragement |

Do not make masculinity, femininity, marriage, parenthood, income, leadership, or emotional style an extra condition of acceptance. Apply the same gospel with different proposed examples, practices, and facilitation choices.
