## 1. Biblical Exegesis

- **1:10 — Whose approval?** After placing even himself under the gospel, Paul refuses the charge that his message is calculated to persuade or please people. “Still” looks back to a former way of living. A servant of Christ does not aim the message at human approval.
- **1:11–12 — Where did this gospel come from?** It is not of human origin. Paul did not receive it from a human source or learn it as his own construction. It came by a revelation of Jesus Christ. This is apostolic witness. It does not teach that believers today bypass Scripture for a private revelation.
- **1:13–14 — What did zeal produce?** Paul persecuted God’s church and tried to destroy it, while advancing beyond his contemporaries and showing extreme zeal for ancestral traditions. Jewish identity and God’s law are not the enemy. Religious seriousness can oppose the gospel.
- **1:15–17 — Who acted?** God set him apart, called him by grace, and was pleased to reveal his Son in him so that he would preach Christ among the Gentiles. Paul did not immediately consult anyone or go to the earlier apostles in Jerusalem. Arabia and Damascus defend the source of his gospel. They are not a rule that believers should avoid the church.
- **1:18–20 — What contact did he have?** After three years he stayed fifteen days with Cephas and saw James. The oath underlines that he is not inventing independence. The contact was real and limited.
- **1:21–24 — What did the churches do with the story?** Unknown by face in Judea, he was heard of as the persecutor who now preaches the faith he tried to destroy. They glorified God because of him. The story ends in worship, not in Paul’s managed reputation.

## 2. Theological Synthesis

**Central idea:** The gospel Paul preaches was given by God in the revelation of his Son; human approval and religious achievement did not produce it, and a life changed by grace leads people to glorify God.

Christ remains the center: God reveals his Son, and the faith Paul now preaches is the faith he once tried to destroy. Grace calls before Paul offers a record. The Gentile mission in verse 16 keeps this personal story inside the letter’s larger question of how the nations belong to God.

People-pleasing and zeal need to stay distinct from faithful courage and genuine conviction. Telling the truth about Christ is not a license to be harsh. Relating to the other apostles, which the next chapter will open, is not a contradiction of verse 17.

No new doctrinal position is proposed. The church’s existing account of grace, Scripture, repentance, and life together is the frame. Nothing here requires Russ’s approval unless a later draft turns Paul’s apostolic revelation into a pattern for private revelation, or turns verse 17 into a rejection of shared oversight.

## 3. Proposed Formation Goals

These continue Session 1’s reviewed angles and apply them to this passage. They are proposals for this gathering, not claims about all men or women. Paul has not yet confirmed them.

| Dimension | Men’s proposal | Women’s proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Belonging | A man can tell the truth in the room without managing how competent or certain he appears | A woman can belong without offering the more admirable story |
| Beholding | See that Paul’s zeal and advancement were not the source of the gospel; God called him by grace and revealed his Son | See that the churches glorified God because of Paul; the story points to grace |
| Becoming | Name one place where approval is steering speech or silence, and tell one true thing to a brother | Tell the truth about mercy received, and encourage another woman by giving God glory for his work in her |
