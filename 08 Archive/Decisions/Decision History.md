## Original Purpose

The Decision Log is a governance tool, not a meeting transcript. It helps future leaders understand:

1. What has been decided?
2. What evidence supports that decision?
3. What ministry work is affected?
4. What still requires a decision?

Only record decisions that materially change the ministry's design, leadership, operations, curriculum, or governing documentation. Routine tasks, meeting notes, implementation progress, and minor content edits belong in their respective ministry or roadmap documents.

Optimize for long-term maintainability over completeness. Most entries should use the four-field default. Add context, rationale, or boundaries only when a foundational decision would otherwise be misunderstood.

### DEC-024 — Distinct Formation Emphases Across Ministry Environments

- **Decision:** As an internal interpretive aid for ministry development, Golden City Church's three launch environments serve different ministry purposes through distinct formation emphases:
  - **City Groups:** approximately 80% fellowship and 20% structured discipleship—primarily shared life and relational time, with the weekly recap providing further discussion questions from the weekend message.

  - **Classes:** approximately 20% fellowship and 80% structured discipleship—an intentional space for both intellectual formation and spiritual formation.
- **Status:** Superseded by DEC-025

- **Implications:** Internal ministry design, leader preparation, curriculum, gathering flow, and evaluation should preserve the distinct purpose of each environment rather than making every gathering feel like the same program. Public communication should use qualitative descriptions rather than the ratios.
- **Context:** The ministry already distinguished distributed City Groups, centralized Collectives, and Classes. The percentages clarify their relative emphasis and help leaders design gatherings consistently.
- **Rationale:** People need complementary environments for friendship and belonging, balanced community and discipleship, and concentrated intellectual and spiritual formation.
- **Boundary:** The percentages are internal ministry-development heuristics, not timed quotas, numerical performance targets, public Groups Ministry language, or a claim that fellowship is outside discipleship. All three environments should include belonging, beholding, and becoming; they differ in emphasis, not in whether formation occurs.

- **Subsequent direction:** DEC-025 supersedes this three-environment framing and its percentage-based development shorthand. The historical rationale remains preserved here.
