# Ministry Model

## Document Status

The ministry purpose, the three complementary ministry environments, and their distinct formation emphases are **confirmed**. The numeric shorthand recorded in [[ministry-context|Ministry Context]] is an internal interpretive aid for ministry development, not public Groups Ministry language. Specific group assignments, schedules, locations, capacities, and remaining readiness details remain unconfirmed unless recorded in `decision-log.md`.

## Approved Ministry Purpose

> Groups provide the primary environment for Christ-centered community, pastoral care, prayer, mutual encouragement, and everyday discipleship. Classes provide intentional environments for deeper biblical, theological, and ministry formation, equipping people to live faithfully, serve the church, and help form others. Together they support Golden City Church in seeing people forge their faith, find freedom in Christ, and follow Jesus fully for the flourishing of the church and the witness of the gospel in the city.

## One Ministry, Three Complementary Environments

City Groups, Men's and Women's Collectives, and Classes are not disconnected departments or interchangeable programs. They share:

- Golden City Church's identity and pastoral oversight
- The Belonging, Beholding, Becoming framework
- A commitment to Jesus, Scripture, the Holy Spirit, people, service, generosity, and flourishing
- A participant pathway toward faithful life in Christ
- A leadership-development ecosystem

The environments below are described qualitatively. Their corresponding internal shorthand is a design heuristic rather than a timed quota. “Discipleship” names the more structured formation portion of the gathering; fellowship itself is also formative Christian life.

### City Groups — Fellowship-Centered Community

City Groups are the most relationally weighted environment. They are weekly, geographically distributed groups that primarily create room for shared life, friendship, hospitality, prayer, mutual care, and belonging close to home.

The structured portion uses a **weekly recap** with further discussion questions from the weekend message. The recap extends Sunday teaching into conversation and response without turning the gathering into a class.

City Groups contribute:

- Christ-centered community
- Hospitality and belonging
- Pastoral awareness and mutual care
- Prayer and encouragement
- Scripture-centered conversation connected to the weekend message
- Everyday discipleship
- Service and local gospel witness
- Contexts in which gifts and emerging leaders can be recognized

Exact communities, leaders, hosts, locations, and capacities remain subject to launch readiness.

### Men's and Women's Collectives — Community and Discipleship in Balance

Collectives are larger, centrally located, semester-based environments. They hold community and intentional biblical or formative engagement in balance.

Collectives contribute:

- relationships and shared identity among men or women;
- hospitality, conversation, and mutual encouragement;
- sustained engagement with a biblical book, curriculum, or formative theme;
- prayer and practical response;
- a bridge between broad church participation and more concentrated formation.

The balanced emphasis means a Collective should not become either a social event with a brief devotional attached or a classroom with incidental conversation.

### Classes — Concentrated Intellectual and Spiritual Formation

Classes are the most concentrated formation environment. Fellowship supports the learning community, while most of the gathering is intentionally directed toward both intellectual formation and spiritual formation.

Classes provide:

- Deeper biblical and theological formation
- Biblical interpretation
- Spiritual and ministry practices
- Leadership development
- Preparation to serve the church
- Equipping people to help form others

Intellectual formation should deepen understanding of Scripture and theology. Spiritual formation should lead toward worship, prayer, repentance, obedience, freedom in Christ, service, and faithful practice. Classes must not become detached academic programs.

Planted is confirmed as the first planned class, with Paul as instructor for a nine- or ten-week course leading to Christmas 2026. Exact dates, location, capacity, materials, participant path, and final curriculum/readiness approval remain open. God With Us and Covenant, Kingdom, People of God remain curriculum scaffolds rather than approved launch offerings.

Future curriculum may address covenant, temple, ecclesiology, Christian metaphysics and metanarrative, discipleship, spiritual formation, and biblical theology. These subjects are **future possibilities**, not an approved launch catalogue.

## How the Environments Reinforce One Another

- City Groups provide sustained relational belonging and a place to embody the weekend message in everyday life.
- Collectives combine wider fellowship with a more sustained formative focus than City Groups.
- Classes provide concentrated learning and practice that can strengthen both City Groups and Collectives.
- City Groups and Collectives surface questions, needs, gifts, and emerging leaders that Classes may help equip.
- All three environments lead toward prayer, service, mission, and helping form others.

No environment is a bureaucratic prerequisite for another. People may participate in the environment that best serves their current need, and movement among them should remain relational rather than programmatic.

## Participant Journey

The following is a **proposed operating pathway**, not yet an approved program sequence:

1. A person encounters Golden City Church through a Sunday gathering or relationship.
2. The person receives a clear invitation and simple next step.
3. A ministry leader follows up and helps connect the person appropriately.
4. A City Group or Collective provides an appropriate environment for fellowship, prayer, care, Scripture, and everyday discipleship.
5. Classes provide a more concentrated opportunity for intellectual and spiritual formation.
6. The person grows in faithful participation, freedom in Christ, service, generosity, and gospel witness.
7. Gifts and capacity may be discerned through relationship and participation.
8. Some participants enter apprenticeship or defined ministry responsibility.
9. Mature disciples help form others with or without holding a formal leadership title.

Movement through this journey is relational and formative, not automatic or strictly linear.

## Leader Development Pathway

### Confirmed Initial City Group Pipeline

For the fall 2026 launch season, the first City Group leaders will be identified and developed from within Golden City Church's Launch Team. The Launch Team has been formed through the church's pre-launch gatherings since March 2026 and currently includes more than 50 committed volunteers preparing to become the church's initial ministry teams.

The confirmed launch sequence is:

1. Pre-launch church plant gatherings form the Launch Team.
2. Team Nights on July 28, August 11, and August 25 provide City Group vision, leader sign-ups, discernment, placement conversations, and launch preparation.
3. Potential leaders are prayerfully discerned and invited from within the Launch Team.
4. Approved candidates receive the required training.
5. Leaders and participants are assigned to launch-ready groups.
6. Leaders are commissioned through a practice still requiring pastoral and operational confirmation.
7. Golden City Church launches publicly on September 20, 2026.

Leader sign-up is an expression of interest rather than appointment. Paul owns routine coordination and leader-interest follow-up; Russ or his designated pastor gives final readiness approval. Exact leaders, groups, assignments, training content, and commissioning details remain unresolved until recorded in `decision-log.md`.

This initial internal pipeline does not establish the permanent leadership-development model. Broader public leadership pathways may be developed after launch as pastoral oversight, ministry maturity, and real participant relationships allow.

### Proposed Long-Term Pathway

The following is **proposed** and requires pastoral approval:

1. Participant
2. Engaged contributor
3. Relational and pastoral discernment
4. Apprentice
5. Bounded, supervised responsibility
6. Approved leader, host, or instructor
7. Experienced leader who helps develop others
8. Coach or trainer when ministry scale and gifting warrant it

Development should consider character, theological alignment, relational trust, faithfulness, capacity, teachability, safe pastoral judgment, demonstrated skill, and explicit approval.

## Ministry Boundaries

- Groups and Classes support but do not replace Sunday worship or pastoral leadership.
- Classes must not become detached academic programs.
- Groups must not become inward-looking social circles.
- Leadership development must not become automatic promotion.
- The ideal future model must not determine launch scope apart from actual capacity.
- Confidential pastoral information must be kept out of this general repository.
- Planning Center records, leader roles, Church Center listings, and enrollment settings support approved ministry operations but do not replace pastoral discernment, appointment, safeguarding, or launch-readiness approval.
