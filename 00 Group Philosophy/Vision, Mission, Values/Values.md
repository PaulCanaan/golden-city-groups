# Values

## Confirmed church values

Golden City Church's values are:

- **Jesus is our Pursuit**
- **Scripture is our Authority**
- **The Holy Spirit is our Power**
- **People are our Heart**
- **Service is our Privilege**
- **Generosity is our Lifestyle**
- **Flourishing is our Goal**

These are confirmed church values. The interpretations and implications below explain how they orient the Groups and Classes Ministry; they do not establish unapproved ministry practices.

## How the values shape the ministry

### Jesus is our Pursuit

**Interpretation:** Jesus is the center and goal of Christian formation. Community, learning, leadership, and ministry growth are good only as they remain ordered toward knowing, worshiping, and following him (Colossians 1:15–20, 28).

**Practical implication:** Groups, Classes, and leadership development should direct attention toward Christ rather than making belonging, knowledge, influence, or activity the final measure of maturity.

### Scripture is our Authority

**Interpretation:** Scripture governs the church's doctrine, teaching, correction, imagination, and training in faithful life (2 Timothy 3:14–17). Theological interest and pastoral experience remain accountable to God's Word and the church's approved beliefs.

**Practical implication:** Group material, class curriculum, and leader training should be evaluated for fidelity to Scripture, not merely usefulness, novelty, or personal preference.

### The Holy Spirit is our Power

**Interpretation:** The church depends on the Holy Spirit for new life, transformation, unity, gifts, prayer, courage, and witness. Spiritual fruit cannot be produced by curriculum, leadership ability, or organizational strength alone (Acts 1:8; Galatians 5:22–25).

**Practical implication:** Ministry design should acknowledge dependence on the Spirit while leaving specific practices related to gifts, discernment, and prayer ministry open for pastoral direction.

### People are our Heart

**Interpretation:** People are neighbors to love, persons made in God's image, and members of one body—not attendance units, volunteer capacity, or evidence of ministry success (Matthew 22:37–40; 1 Corinthians 12:12–27).

**Practical implication:** Systems, schedules, teaching, and leadership structures should serve the formation and care of people rather than treating people as servants of the system.

### Service is our Privilege

**Interpretation:** Jesus forms his people in the pattern of his own self-giving service (Mark 10:42–45). Service is grateful participation in Christ's life and mission, not merely a way to staff church activity.

**Practical implication:** Opportunities for responsibility should be framed around love, gifting, character, faithfulness, and the good of others rather than pressure or status.

### Generosity is our Lifestyle

**Interpretation:** The gospel creates a generous people. Generosity includes material giving, but also time, attention, hospitality, encouragement, patience, gifts, and shared life (2 Corinthians 8:9; 9:6–8).

**Practical implication:** Groups and Classes should resist a consumer posture by inviting people to receive grace and, as they mature, contribute to the good and formation of others.

### Flourishing is our Goal

**Interpretation:** Christian flourishing is life ordered toward Christ: reconciliation with God, growth in holiness and love, participation in his people, faithful use of gifts, service of neighbor, and hope in God's kingdom (John 15:1–11).

**Practical implication:** Ministry health should not be reduced to comfort, activity, attendance, or institutional expansion. A fuller evaluation approach belongs in [[Metrics]].

## The values belong together

The values are most faithful when they interpret and correct one another. Pursuing Jesus without submitting to Scripture can become projection; appealing to Scripture without dependence on the Holy Spirit can become information without transformation; speaking of the Spirit without attention to Jesus and Scripture can become untethered from the church's center and authority.

The remaining values show the communal shape of this life. People are loved rather than used. Service is received as participation in Christ rather than a path to status. Generosity loosens the hold of possessiveness and makes room for shared life. Flourishing names the good toward which these convictions move: people rooted in Christ, growing in love and holiness, contributing their gifts, and bearing faithful witness together.

This integration matters for both Groups and Classes. A group may offer warm relationships but still fail to direct people toward Christ and Scripture. A class may teach accurate content but still fail to cultivate prayer, love, service, or dependence on the Spirit. Leadership may accomplish visible work while neglecting the people entrusted to its care. The values give the ministry a shared way to notice these imbalances without turning formation into a checklist.

## Relationship to Belonging, Beholding, Becoming

| Formation emphasis | Values that give it particular shape | Ministry focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Belonging** | People; Generosity | Hospitality, care, participation, and shared life in Christ |
| **Beholding** | Jesus; Scripture; the Holy Spirit | Attention to Christ through Scripture, prayer, worship, teaching, and the Spirit's work |
| **Becoming** | Service; Generosity; Flourishing | Christlike character, faithful living, service, witness, and helping form others |

This mapping identifies emphases, not exclusive categories. Every value touches all three dimensions, and Belonging, Beholding, and Becoming continually reinforce one another.

## Ministry interpretation and boundaries

The seven value statements are **confirmed church language**. Their application to curriculum, leadership, group rhythms, spiritual gifts, generosity, service expectations, and evaluation requires ongoing pastoral discernment. Unresolved questions remain governed by `docs/decision-log.md`.

The values should shape ministry judgment without becoming a scorecard. They help the ministry ask whether its structures remain centered on Jesus, faithful to Scripture, dependent on the Spirit, attentive to people, oriented toward service and generosity, and directed toward faithful flourishing.
