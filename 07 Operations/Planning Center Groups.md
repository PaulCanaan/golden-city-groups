## Purpose

Planning Center should help Golden City Church remember people, respond clearly, support leaders, and maintain trustworthy ministry records. It supports the ministry described in [[Groups Overview]]; it does not replace prayer, relationship, pastoral discernment, safeguarding, or the work of the Holy Spirit.

- **Belonging:** accurate information, timely responses, and dependable rosters help people enter real community without being overlooked.
- **Beholding:** events and approved resources help keep Scripture, prayer, worship, and the Sunday message connected to group life.
- **Becoming:** participation may prompt care, service, and leadership development, but software activity is not a measure of spiritual maturity.

## How the System Fits Together

Planning Center is a connected suite. **People** is the shared people database. **Groups** is the ministry's operational record. **Church Center** is the participant-facing web and mobile experience.

| Ministry need | Planning Center area | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Person and contact details | People | Maintain one profile per person and avoid duplicates. |
| Launch Team leader interest | Planning Center interest form | Paul supports submission, owns intake, and communicates immediate next steps under DEC-026. |
| Assigned follow-up | People Workflow, if approved | Track operational next steps without treating a card as appointment. |
| Public discovery | Church Center | Show accurate, launch-ready offerings and clear next steps. |
| Join requests and rosters | Groups | Review requests and maintain leaders, members, and visitors. |
| Meetings and RSVPs | Groups events | Schedule confirmed gatherings and optional reminders or RSVPs. |
| Attendance | Groups events | Record participation and notice possible care needs. |
| Communication | Groups email and chat | Send approved information and support appropriate conversation. |
| Guides and files | Group resources | Share approved curriculum and resources. |
| Reporting | Groups reports and People Lists | Review aggregate membership, events, and attendance in context. |

### System-of-record rule

Planning Center should be the operational roster of record for approved offerings. Do not maintain permanent parallel rosters in this repository, personal spreadsheets, or unofficial contact lists. Do not copy exports containing names, contact details, addresses, rosters, or pastoral information into this vault.

## What Groups Can Do

- Apply shared settings through group types.
- List approved offerings in Church Center and receive join requests.
- Maintain leader, member, visitor, and attendance responsibilities.
- Schedule recurring events, optional RSVPs, reminders, and attendance requests.
- Share approved resources.
- Email selected members or the whole group.
- Enable group chat where appropriate.
- Report group health, membership, events, and attendance.
- Duplicate or archive records for seasonal administration.

### Important limits

- A join request is not membership until an authorized person approves it.
- Leaders count toward a group's enrollment limit.
- Authorized users can add people to closed or full groups; technical ability does not authorize routine capacity overrides.
- Leaders should invite people or use a Church Center link or QR code. Wider People-database search should remain off unless expressly approved.
- Groups email supports up to 500 recipients at once. Larger sends should follow the approved People/Mailchimp process.
- A group can have up to 100 chat conversations; the oldest is removed when the next one begins. Chat is not a permanent archive.
- Children under 13 cannot participate in chat. Children's attendance requires the approved Check-Ins and safeguarding process.
- Confidential settings restrict member visibility and may remove chat or member-contact functions. Test the exact member experience before launch.
- RSVP and attendance are different records. Do not infer presence from an RSVP.

## Roles and Access

Use the least access required for the approved responsibility.

| Responsibility | Access pattern | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Account security and subscriptions | Organization administrator | Keep rare and ensure continuity. |
| All Groups operations | Paul as ministry point person, with the approved Groups permission | Confirm the exact permission and name a backup. |
| A defined family of groups | Group-type manager, if needed | Use only if ministry scale warrants it. |
| One group | Group leader | Add only after appointment and training. |
| Attendance for one group | Attendance permission | Does not confer leadership authority. |
| A form or workflow task | Specific collaborator | Limit access to the assigned process. |
| Ordinary participation | Church Center member | No administrative access. |

Before granting access:

1. Search for the person's existing profile.
2. Choose the narrowest sufficient permission.
3. Explain responsibilities, privacy boundaries, and expected use.
4. Apply the church's two-step-verification requirements.
5. Test the person's actual view rather than relying on an administrator view.
6. Remove or reduce access promptly when responsibilities change.

## End-to-End Operating Instruction

### 1. Establish ownership

Paul is the confirmed operational point person for the ministry and Planning Center. DEC-026 confirms his Planning Center admin access for leader-interest form intake and his ownership of initial applicant follow-up. Russ owns the public participant system, with Paul as backup, under DEC-032. Leader-interest backup coverage, least-privilege settings beyond the approved uses, response standards, participant join-request workflow, and event/attendance ownership remain unresolved. Paul recommends an offering as ready; Russ or a pastor explicitly designated by Russ gives final readiness approval. Participant-data access follows DEC-028 and the remaining implementation choices in PRO-030.

### 2. Configure the approved group type

Use only existing or approved launch types. Review:

- title, description, image, and contact person;
- listing, map, schedule, and location visibility;
- enrollment method, close date, capacity, and alerts;
- event reminders, attendance reminders, and RSVP behavior;
- member confidentiality and contact visibility;
- whether leaders or all members may start chat;
- leader database access, with the default remaining **off**;
- resources visible to leaders or members.

Create a temporary test group when changing important defaults. View and test it as an ordinary Church Center user, then archive the test record.

### 3. Handle Launch Team leader interest

For Fall 2026, initial City Group leaders are identified, invited, discerned, and trained from within the Launch Team through the July 28, August 11, and August 25 Team Nights under DEC-018 and DEC-019. Do not publish a general public leader application for this pipeline.

During the August Team Nights, Paul helps interested Launch Team members access and submit the appropriate Planning Center interest form. Paul receives the submissions through his admin access, reviews and organizes them, and emails applicants to arrange interviews and communicate immediate next steps. The form remains limited to the Launch Team pipeline and must state that interest is not appointment or assignment.

The approved workflow begins:

`Team Night form submission → Paul reviews and organizes → Paul emails applicant and arranges interview`

Use the working copy in [[Communication#Leader-Interest Communication Kit]] for the Team Night invitation, form confirmation, and applicant follow-up. Fall 2026 focuses on Launch Team members interested in hosting City Groups. The standardized interview format and duration remain unresolved.

The remaining workflow is still approval-dependent:

`standardized interview → leader assessment → safeguarding review → approval decision → communicate outcome → training → group assignment → appointment → Planning Center leader access → commissioning → CoJourner and post-launch support`

Keep workflow notes brief: status, owner, and immediate next step. The exact approved fields, consent, retention, backup, interview records, and assessment documentation remain unresolved. Do not store background-check details, confession, diagnoses, unverified allegations, or unnecessary pastoral narrative in a form, workflow, group record, or this repository. A completed workflow does not appoint a leader, and Paul's intake ownership does not grant final appointment authority.

See [[Forms]] and [[05 Leadership/Group Leaders]].

### 4. Build the group record

Keep the record **unlisted** with enrollment **closed** while details remain provisional. Enter only confirmed information:

- approved name and public description;
- accountable ministry owner;
- appointed leader;
- verified schedule and location;
- realistic capacity and enrollment method;
- approved Church Center contact path;
- actual season events, including holidays and breaks;
- approved reminder, RSVP, attendance, resource, privacy, and chat settings.

Add a person as leader only after the approved appointment and training process.

### 5. Test and publish

Complete [[Launch Checklist]]. Using a non-admin profile, test:

1. the exact public link on phone and desktop;
2. the public name, description, schedule, location, and capacity;
3. the join request and confirmation;
4. delivery to the correct owner and backup;
5. approval, welcome, and member access;
6. events, resources, communication, and attendance;
7. full, closed, redirection, and correction paths.

Paul completes the operational review and recommends readiness. Record approval from Russ or his designated pastor before listing the group or opening enrollment. A website button should point to the specific tested destination.

### 6. Process join requests

The assigned owner reviews each request within the approved response window.

- **Accept/place:** update membership and send the next meeting details.
- **Clarify:** contact the person before deciding.
- **Waitlist:** state what will happen next and when they will hear again.
- **Redirect:** offer a specific suitable option and a human handoff.
- **Decline:** follow the approved pastoral process for an eligibility, safety, scope, or capacity reason.

Confirm date, time, location or meeting link, and what to expect. Keep request and membership status accurate. Do not override capacity as routine practice or leave a person at a dead end.

A one-business-day response during public signup windows is a **proposed target** and must not be promised until an owner and backup are assigned. See [[Forms]].

### 7. Operate the group weekly

The appointed leader or approved recorder:

1. reviews new requests and confirms the next event;
2. communicates genuine schedule changes through the approved channel;
3. keeps the roster accurate and distinguishes members from visitors;
4. records attendance after the gathering;
5. submits the report within the approved window;
6. follows up relationally where appropriate;
7. moves sensitive pastoral or safeguarding concerns into the church-approved escalation process.

An unsubmitted attendance report is not a zero-attendance event. Attendance may prompt a caring conversation, but it does not measure Christian faithfulness. Do not put counseling, health, prayer, abuse, crisis, or safeguarding details in attendance notes or chat. See [[Attendance]].

### 8. Communicate appropriately

- Use **event reminders** for confirmed meeting details and RSVP requests.
- Use **group email** for clear information that should reach members' primary email addresses.
- Use **chat** for appropriate group conversation where enabled.
- Use **direct contact** for one person's pastoral matter.
- Use the church's approved urgent process for emergencies and safeguarding.

Use one clear action, include relevant date/time/location information, verify recipients, and obtain permission before sharing another person's prayer request or circumstances. Do not use chat for emergency response, mandatory reports, counseling records, conflict documentation, or discernment notes. See [[Communication]].

### 9. Review ministry health

During public signup, review requests, broken links, capacity, public details, and notification delivery daily. During the first launch month, review attendance submission, roster accuracy, schedule changes, and leader support weekly. After stabilization, use an approved monthly rhythm.

Useful questions include:

- Are any requests or participant transitions waiting for a human response?
- Are public offerings accurate and connected to a working next step?
- Which events lack submitted attendance?
- Where do schedule, location, childcare, accessibility, capacity, or leader supply limit belonging?
- Which leaders need encouragement or practical support?
- What stories show people belonging, beholding Christ, and becoming faithful disciples beyond what the numbers reveal?

Use aggregate Groups reports and approved People Lists. Numbers should prompt prayer, care, correction, and planning rather than spiritual ranking. See [[07 Operations/Metrics]].

### 10. Close the season

1. Confirm the final gathering and transition communication.
2. Offer approved next steps and identify people needing personal transition care.
3. Resolve outstanding requests and stop future events.
4. Review roster and resource handling under the approved retention policy.
5. Archive the group when appropriate; do not delete history or reuse a record with old members without an approved reset process.
6. Remove obsolete permissions and leader roles.

## Approval Gates

The following remain launch blockers:

- Planning Center administrator, join-request owner, backup, and readiness approver;
- leader permission level and wider People-database access;
- participant fields, consent, access, correction, retention, and deletion practices;
- confidentiality, chat, direct-message, and event-note boundaries;
- safeguarding for minors, vulnerable people, private homes, and childcare;
- response window, placement, capacity, and waitlist ownership;
- final approval for each offering.

Record approved cross-cutting decisions in `00 Dashboard/Decision Log.md` rather than silently turning a proposal into policy.
