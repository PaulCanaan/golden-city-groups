# Group Leader Conversation Questions

**Golden City Church — Groups Ministry**

**Purpose:** A pastoral discernment guide for conversations with prospective group leaders and hosts.

## Document Status

**Existing canonical operational question bank; use remains subject to the approved conversation and privacy boundaries.** Use with [[06 Resources/Leader Development/Leader Conversation Guide|Leader Conversation Guide]]. These prompts do not authorize appointment, establish a scoring rubric, or settle the still-open interview format, outcome record, role-specific criteria, safeguarding, or pastoral-escalation implementation.

> The goal is not to conduct a job interview or find perfect leaders. The goal is to hear the person's story, understand their desire to serve, and discern together whether leading, co-leading, or hosting a group is a healthy next step.

## Conversation Posture

Approach the interview as a relational and pastoral conversation rather than a rigid questionnaire.

The central discernment question is:

**Can we trust and equip this person to help create a Christ-centered community where people belong, behold Jesus, and become more like Him?**

Not every question needs to be asked. Use the questions below as prompts and follow the conversation naturally.

---

## 1. Story & Motivation

**Goal:** Understand the person's relationship with Jesus, experience of Christian community, and motivation for leadership.

1. Tell me a little about your story with Jesus. How did you come to faith, and what has God been doing in your life recently?
2. What has your experience with Christian community or small groups been like?
3. What made you interested in leading or hosting a group at Golden City?
4. What are you hoping God might do through a group you help lead?

**Listen for:** genuine relationship with Jesus, self-awareness, teachability, servant-hearted motivation, and desire for community rather than status or control.

---

## 2. Belonging — Creating Christ-Centered Community

**Goal:** Discern the person's capacity for hospitality, relational care, inclusion, and reconciliation.

5. Think about a group where you really felt like you belonged. What made that community feel that way?
6. If someone came to your group who was new to church, skeptical about Christianity, socially uncomfortable, or simply very different from you, how would you help them feel welcomed?
7. How would you respond if someone consistently stayed quiet or seemed disconnected from the rest of the group?
8. What would you do if two people in your group were experiencing tension or conflict?

**Listen for:** hospitality, patience, humility, curiosity, emotional maturity, healthy boundaries, and an instinct toward reconciliation.

---

## 3. Beholding — Pointing People Toward Jesus

**Goal:** Discern whether the leader's spiritual center of gravity is Jesus and whether they can facilitate Scripture-centered conversation without needing to be the expert.

9. If someone asked you, “What does it actually mean to follow Jesus?” how would you answer?
10. What role do Scripture and prayer currently play in your own life?
11. How comfortable are you facilitating a conversation about Scripture when you may not know every answer?
12. What would you do if someone asked a theological or biblical question during group that you couldn't answer?

**Listen for:** dependence on Jesus, love for Scripture, humility, willingness to learn, and comfort saying, “I don't know, but let's explore that together.”

---

## 4. Becoming — Helping People Grow

**Goal:** Understand how the person thinks about discipleship, spiritual formation, and helping others take meaningful next steps.

13. What does spiritual growth look like to you?
14. How can a group help someone grow as a disciple of Jesus?
15. Who has helped you grow spiritually, and what did they do that was helpful?
16. What are you currently learning or growing in yourself?
17. How would you respond to someone who regularly attends the group but seems hesitant to take any next steps in following Jesus?

**Listen for:** a formation-oriented understanding of groups, patience with people's growth, intentional discipleship, and recognition that leaders remain disciples themselves.

---

## 5. Leadership, Care & Pastoral Judgment

**Goal:** Discern humility, relational maturity, healthy boundaries, and willingness to involve church leadership when situations exceed the leader's role.

18. Imagine someone shares something deeply personal or painful during group. What would you do in that moment?
19. What kinds of situations do you think a group leader can appropriately walk through with someone, and when should they ask church leadership for help?
20. How do you normally respond when someone gives you correction or feedback?
21. Tell me about a time you experienced conflict with someone you were serving, working, or doing ministry with. How did you handle it?
22. What do you think healthy leadership looks like in a small-group environment?

**Listen for:** care, humility, appropriate boundaries, teachability, willingness to ask for help, and an understanding that group leaders are not expected to function as counselors or pastors in every situation.

> Specific safeguarding and escalation procedures should be taught during leader onboarding. The interview should discern the candidate's instincts and judgment rather than expect prior knowledge of Golden City's procedures.

---

## 6. Practical Readiness

**Goal:** Determine whether leadership is realistic and sustainable in the person's current season.

23. What would leading a group realistically look like within your current work, family, church, and life commitments?
24. Would you prefer to lead, co-lead, or host? Why?
25. Are there particular people, neighborhoods, life stages, or communities you feel especially drawn to serve?
26. What kind of support, resources, or training from Golden City would help you lead well?
27. Is there anything about your current season or circumstances that you think we should know as we discern whether this is the right time for you to lead?

---

## Closing the Conversation

Before finishing:

- Give the candidate an opportunity to ask questions.
- Explain the next step in the discernment/onboarding process.
- Avoid making an immediate commitment if further discernment or consultation is needed.
- Thank them for their willingness to serve and invest in people.

A helpful closing question:

**What questions do you have for me about groups, leadership, or what Golden City would expect from you?**

---

## Post-Conversation Discernment

Do not score every question individually. After the conversation, consider the candidate across five areas:

| Area | Discernment Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Jesus** | Is this person actively following Jesus and continuing to grow? |
| **Character** | Are they humble, teachable, trustworthy, and relationally mature? |
| **People** | Can they welcome, listen to, care for, and build relationships with different kinds of people? |
| **Formation** | Do they understand groups as environments for discipleship and spiritual formation, not merely social gatherings? |
| **Readiness** | Do they currently have the capacity, willingness, and support needed to lead well? |

### Recommended Outcome

Choose one:

- **Ready** — Proceed toward leader onboarding.
- **Ready with Support** — Proceed with identified coaching, co-leadership, or development needs.
- **Not Yet** — Encourage continued participation and development before leadership.
- **Further Conversation Needed** — Additional information, pastoral conversation, or leadership discernment is needed before making a decision.

---

## Ministry Framework

Group leadership should ultimately serve the Groups Ministry's formation movement:

**Belonging → Beholding → Becoming**

- **Belonging:** Creating environments where people are known, welcomed, and connected in Christ-centered community.
- **Beholding:** Helping people encounter Jesus through Scripture, prayer, worship, testimony, and life together.
- **Becoming:** Helping people increasingly follow Jesus, grow in Christlike character, practice their faith, and participate in God's mission.

The interview is therefore not merely a gatekeeping step. It is an early step in identifying, developing, and equipping people who can cultivate healthy communities of discipleship.
